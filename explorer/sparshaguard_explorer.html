      Whole-body study · 11 October 2026 · fig 10–12

      Touches anywhere on her body

      The sweep sees a hand at the wrist. A touch on her shoulder or back reaches the watch through the same weak link as walking past a wall, so the sweep can't tell them apart. A passive static channel on the same electrode [18, 21, 22] sees the moment another body touches her, anywhere; the wrist accelerometer tells a grab that pulls her from a brush.

      Passive body-potential channel simulated in time · fig 17–18

      When two people at different voltages touch, they share charge [21] and her whole body's voltage jumps. The floating watch sees a fixed fraction of that jump at its electrode.

      Voltage difference between the two bodies
      0.01 V1 V1,000 V
      Amplifier input

      step at the wrist electrode

      Walking charges a person to hundreds or thousands of volts on vinyl (250–12,000 V [17]; up to ±20 kV in dry air [32]), so a 1 V difference is modest; Indian floors are not measured yet. Her own steps make bigger changes: lifting a foot drops body capacitance from about 160 to 100 pF [40], so her voltage rises 1.6× at every step. But steps and arm movements take 50–300 ms, while charge sharing at a touch settles in well under a microsecond (body model 150 pF, 330 Ω [54]). The firmware keeps only sharp steps that stand out from the signal around them: in 4.5 simulated hours of walking, bus and desk life at 1–1,000 V, with the foot-lift swings included, it found no false steps. Two people at the same voltage still give no signal.

      Fused watch: where a grab is caught simulated

      Time-domain simulation (passive_stream.py): static channel at 500 Hz, wrist accelerometer, active sweep; walking, bus and desk; bootstrapped input + ADS1115.

      Grabs that only hold (about 30 %, an estimate) give no pulling and are missed away from the wrist. A friend holding her wrist still prompts; her PIN cancels it. Motion rates and street body voltages are estimates until Gate 1.

      Signs of assault in Guard Mode twin · estimated

      Besides touch, the watch reads its accelerometer, gyroscope, heart-rate sensor and microphone. These run in the digital twin; their thresholds are estimates, because no published wrist data for assaults was found.

      SignalWhat the watch doesSimulated result
      Struggling to get free
      accelerometer + gyroscope      		5 or more jerks over 2 g in 4 s, at irregular intervals, with the wrist turning faster than 400°/s: asks for the PIN even if no touch was felt
      Running
      accelerometer      		Fast, regular arm swing (above ~0.5 g; running is above 0.6 g at the wrist [43]): not an alarm by itself, but while she runs any touch asks for the PIN at once
      MicrophoneTurns on when the PIN prompt starts. The correct PIN deletes the recording; no PIN, a wrong PIN or the duress PIN sends it with the alertShown in the twin
      GPSLive location goes with every alert, also the silent duress alertShown in the twin
      Heart rateConfirmation only: rises too slowly to start an alarm, so the alert reports it against her usual rateShown in the twin

      Running and her own quick gestures are what make motion-only alarms fail, so the watch looks for irregular, repeated, fast movement. A published smartwatch study classified scripted punches and shoves with 99.6 % accuracy [34]; fall detectors in daily use reach 0.025–0.3 false alarms per hour [33, 56]; real assaults are messier, and Gate 1 must record them safely (staged).
