    Why, in plain words

    What the touch changes in the circuit (the small codes name the circuit branch, for engineers).

      Whole-body study · 11 October 2026 · fig 10–12

      Touches anywhere on her body

      The sweep sees a hand at the wrist. A touch on her shoulder or back reaches the watch through the same weak link as walking past a wall, so the sweep can't tell them apart. A passive static channel on the same electrode sees the moment another body touches her, anywhere; the wrist accelerometer tells a grab that pulls her from a brush.

      Passive body-potential channel simulated in time · fig 17–18

      When two people at different voltages touch, they share charge and her whole body's voltage jumps. The floating watch sees a fixed fraction of that jump at its electrode.

      Voltage difference between the two bodies
      0.01 V1 V1,000 V

      Walking charges a person to hundreds or thousands of volts on vinyl (Desco: 250–12,000 V), so a 1 V difference is modest; Indian floors are not measured yet. Her own footsteps and arm movements make bigger changes, but they take 20–300 ms; a contact settles in under 1 ms. The firmware keeps only sharp steps that stand out from the signal around them: in simulated hours of walking, bus and desk life at 1–1,000 V it found no false steps. Two people at the same voltage still give no signal.

      Fused watch: where a grab is caught simulated

      Time-domain simulation (passive_stream.py): static channel at 500 Hz, wrist accelerometer, active sweep; walking, bus and desk; bootstrapped input + ADS1115.

      Grabs that only hold (about 30 %, an estimate) give no pulling and are missed away from the wrist. A friend holding her wrist still prompts; her PIN cancels it. Motion rates and street body voltages are estimates until Gate 1.
